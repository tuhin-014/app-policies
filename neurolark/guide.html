What the games do, honestly

Neurolark is a short daily habit for practising memory, focus, words, numbers and speaking. Here is what that practice is, what the research says about it, and what else helps.

What you practise

Memory

Holding things in mind for a few seconds and recalling them in order: patterns, lists, numbers and words.

Try it in: Tile Recall, Shopping List, Number Span

Attention

Keeping your focus on one thing while ignoring another, and spotting small differences.

Try it in: Color Clash, Odd One Out, Signal Watch

Speed & math

Everyday mental arithmetic: totals, change, tips and fractions.

Try it in: Quick Sums, Tip Percent, Missing Sign

Problem solving

Step-by-step reasoning, rules and patterns, with no clock when you want it.

Try it in: Sudoku, Next in Pattern, Code Breaker

Words and speaking

Finding the word you mean, and speaking at a steady pace with fewer fillers.

Try it in: Synonym Sprint, Fluency Sprint, Clear Speech

What the research says

Practice makes you better at what you practise. That part is well established.

Better at the games

Your scores will rise as you play. A large review of brain-training studies found clear gains on the trained tasks themselves [1].

Less carry-over to daily life

The same review found less evidence for gains on closely related tasks, and little for distantly related tasks or everyday thinking [1]. In 2014 a group of scientists convened by Stanford and the Max Planck Institute warned that claims for brain games are frequently exaggerated and at times misleading [2]. In 2016 the US Federal Trade Commission settled with a brain-training company over claims that its games improved everyday performance and protected against decline [3].

So we don't promise it

Neurolark does not claim to improve your memory in daily life, raise your IQ, or prevent, slow or treat any condition, including dementia. Enjoy it as practice.

Memory techniques that work

These are well-studied ways to remember things in real life. The games are a good place to rehearse them.

Test yourself

Trying to recall something builds a stronger memory than reading it again. In one classic study, people who practised recalling a passage remembered more of it a week later than people who re-read it [4]. Close the list and see what you remember.

Try it in: Word Recall, Shopping List

Space it out

A little practice spread over days is remembered far better than one long session. A review of more than 300 experiments found the benefit again and again [5]. That's why the workout is short and daily.

Try it in: the daily workout

Chunk it

Group long strings into a few pieces, like a phone number in three parts.

Try it in: Number Span

Picture a route

Place each item along a walk you know well, then walk it again in your head to recall them. Memory champions use this method, and in a 2017 study people with ordinary memories who practised it for six weeks recalled many more words [6].

Try it in: Shopping List

What helps most beyond the app

The strongest evidence for long-term brain health is about everyday life, not games.

Move your body

Regular aerobic exercise, like brisk walking, is one of the best-studied habits for healthy ageing. In one year-long trial with older adults, walking was linked to a slightly larger hippocampus, a brain area involved in memory [7].

Look after the basics

A major 2024 review lists 14 risk factors linked to dementia that can be changed: less education, hearing loss, high LDL cholesterol, depression, head injury, physical inactivity, diabetes, smoking, high blood pressure, obesity, heavy drinking, social isolation, air pollution and untreated vision loss. It estimates that addressing them could prevent or delay a large share of cases across whole populations [8].

Stay connected and keep learning

Time with people, and learning something genuinely new, are good uses of time too. Neurolark is best as a few minutes alongside them, not instead of them.

Talk to a doctor

If you're worried about your memory or speech, a doctor can help. An app can't tell you what is going on.

Neurolark is for practice and enjoyment. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease or condition, including dementia, ADHD or cognitive decline. Speaking games are not speech therapy.
